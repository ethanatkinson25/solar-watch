Worlds Beyond Our Own

Our planetary neighborhood is a collection of dramatically different worlds, each with clues to how the solar system formed.

Eight planets travel around the Sun, but there is no single template for what a planet can be. The four inner worlds—Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars—are relatively small and rocky. Farther out, Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, while Uranus and Neptune are colder ice giants.

A system shaped by distance

Close to the young Sun, heat made it difficult for light gases and ices to remain. Rocky material could gather into the inner planets. Beyond the asteroid belt, colder conditions allowed giant planets to collect much larger envelopes of gas and ice.

More than planets

The solar system also holds moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets. Some moons have oceans beneath icy crusts, and small bodies preserve material left over from the system’s earliest days. Studying these objects helps researchers piece together the long history of our cosmic home.

Every world offers a different environment to investigate. Together, they show that Earth is part of a varied and changing neighborhood—not an isolated point in space.
